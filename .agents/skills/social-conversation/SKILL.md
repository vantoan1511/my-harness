# Social Conversation

Goal: produce natural human messaging for everyday chat, getting-to-know-you conversations, flirting, dating apps, teasing/banter, follow-ups, asking someone out, restarting conversations, and replying to dry or enthusiastic messages.

## Core principles

### Match the current energy
- Enthusiastic → engage and build on it.
- Playful → play along.
- Brief → usually stay reasonably brief.
- Distant/dry → do not compensate with a paragraph.
- Questions → answer them and, when natural, reciprocate.
- Clear lack of interest → do not pressure or chase.

### Don't over-flirt
Use a mix of genuine curiosity, small jokes, callbacks to earlier details, occasional teasing, and subtle specific compliments. Do not make every message romantic or sexual.

### Tease, don't insult
Playful teasing should feel safe and mutual. Avoid appearance insecurities, sensitive traits, humiliation, guilt-tripping, manipulative jealousy, and pressure.

### Give the conversation somewhere to go
Avoid dead-end replies such as only “haha” or “nice” when a response can naturally react, add something, ask, tease, or open a related topic.

### Don't turn it into an interview
Mix questions with self-disclosure, reactions, opinions, stories, and jokes.

### Don't perform
Avoid pickup-artist scripts, fake scarcity, pretending to be busy, deliberate ignoring, “alpha” posturing, canned flirting, or manipulative tactics.

### Specific compliments beat generic compliments
Prefer something grounded in what the person actually said or did over repetitive generic compliments.

### Match language naturally
Use the person's language and register. Do not force slang, abbreviations, emojis, or a flirting style they do not use.

## Flirting intensity

Treat flirting as a gradual scale:

0. Normal conversation
1. Warm / friendly
2. Playful
3. Clear interest
4. Direct invitation / explicit romantic interest

Do not jump to level 4 without enough conversational context.

## Asking someone out

Keep it simple and concrete:
- suggest a specific activity
- suggest a reasonable time
- leave room for an easy yes or no

If declined, accept it without pressure.

## Interpretation

Do not invent hidden meanings from short messages, emoji, response time, or punctuation. When context is ambiguous, respond to what was actually said rather than pretending certainty about the other person's feelings.

## Reply-option mode

If the user asks “reply sao?”, “nhắn gì?”, or equivalent:
- give 2–4 options at most when multiple tones are useful
- vary intensity when appropriate, e.g. natural / playful / slightly flirty
- explain the difference briefly only when useful
